Harish Kumar shared that **Stanford's CME 295 (Autumn 2025) lecture series on large language models is free to watch online**. The course runs nine lectures, starting at the Transformer architecture and ending at agentic LLMs and current trends. For robotics readers, it's a solid grounding in the language-model machinery behind vision-language-action models and LLM-driven robot planners.

---

| # | Lecture |
|---|---|
| 1 | Transformers |
| 2 | Transformer-Based Models |
| 3 | Large Language Models |
| 4 | LLM Training |
| 5 | LLM Tuning |
| 6 | LLM Reasoning |
| 7 | Agentic LLMs |
| 8 | LLM Evaluation |
| 9 | Recap & Current Trends |

---

## Why it's worth a look

- **Foundations first.** Lectures 1 to 3 cover the Transformer and the model families built on it, the same backbone that most robot foundation models start from.
- **Training and tuning.** Lectures 4 and 5 cover how LLMs are trained and adapted, which maps onto how VLAs get fine-tuned for a specific robot or task.
- **Reasoning and agents.** Lectures 6 and 7 are the most relevant to embodied AI: reasoning models and agentic systems that call tools and plan multi-step tasks.
- **Evaluation.** Lecture 8 is useful for judging claims about model quality, which is just as tricky in robotics benchmarks.

Commenters on the post raised two fair questions: whether the course suits complete beginners, and whether it stays current in a field that moves this fast. I haven't watched the lectures myself, so check the syllabus against your own background before committing time.
